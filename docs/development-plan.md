# Space Explorer: proposed development plan

Reviewed 2026-09-07. This plan defines the milestones and their exit criteria; where the work stands against them is tracked in [progress.md](progress.md). This plan translates the [concept](concept.md) into a proposed prototype and release scope. Added defaults are recommendations, not implemented features or confirmed production commitments. The [assessment](assessment.md) explains the reasoning; the [technical design](technical-design.md) specifies data and behavior contracts; the [technology decision](technology-stack.md) selects the working implementation stack.

## Scope and working assumptions

The platform policy is fixed in [requirements.md](requirements.md): Linux-primary development, native Linux and Windows releases validated on their own operating systems, no Wine or Proton, free approachable 3D tooling, and no Python requirement. Use Godot 4 .NET with C#, an engine-independent generator, and SQLite persistence as the working stack. Initial validation targets x86_64 and the Compatibility renderer, subject to measured hardware and presentation requirements; exact versions are pinned at implementation start.

Start with artifact expeditions, manageable environmental hazards, and private cooperation. Use 3D surface movement in bounded landing regions, with map-based or transitional travel between regions. Full planetary surfaces, seamless space-to-ground flight, orbital simulation, terrain deformation, combat, construction, industrial crafting, and public markets are outside the core release.

Use a home anchor for trading and progression. Travel is hub and spoke: every expedition departs from and returns to the anchor, the ship is always at home or at exactly one destination, and distance and tier are measured from the anchor. Destination controls include a distance control that snaps to the two tiers, environment preference, survey focus, recorded seed, and return-visit selection; continuous distance within a tier and chained travel between discovered systems are M3 options ([decision 0012](decisions/0012-core-travel-model.md)). Preview cost, equipment requirements, hazards, cargo capacity, and preparation/resource estimates without revealing artifact locations or prices.

Preparation time is real generation time on the host with no artificial timer; short tiers come first, and days-long tiers with very large budgets are evaluated at M3. Pricing uses predefined reference generation/recovery effort, an authored tier base plus recipe complexity, rather than elapsed wall-clock time ([decision 0004](decisions/0004-preparation-is-real-generation-time.md)). Both refine the original concept; their reception by players remains a validation question.

## Core-release contents

| Area | Minimum usable result |
| --- | --- |
| Platforms | Linux-primary development with portable tooling; native Linux and Windows builds tested on their respective OSes. Confirm initial CPU architectures, supported OS versions/distributions, and minimum graphics requirements during the prototype. |
| Primitive foundation | Random, validated primitive sets generated and durably stored before worlds; 128-bit pack identifiers with one new pack per generation run; permanent pack-local unsigned 32-bit IDs, compact references, pinned dependencies, and expansion without rewriting saved sets. |
| Navigation | One ship, one home anchor, two distance tiers with the second unlocked with credits, a distance control that snaps to tiers, destination preferences, recorded seeds, and return visits. |
| Worlds | One stellar template family; every accepted destination has at least two explorable planets and one observable life-bearing environment, which may overlap. At least one bounded region of at most 2,048 m per axis per explorable planet ([decision 0017](decisions/0017-region-extent-cap-and-storage-derivation.md)). |
| Exploration | Surface movement, orbital/region map, scanner clues, suit reserves, cargo limits, and emergency recovery. |
| Content | At least three biome rule sets, three artifact families, two recovery challenges, and enough compatible compositions for uniqueness and variety checks. |
| Economy | Catalogue, collection storage, NPC buying/selling, supplies, an equipment improvement, and second-tier travel access. |
| Persistence | Save/load, interrupted-generation resume, atomic transactions, recoverable backups, and an update-compatibility demonstration. |
| Multiplayer | A host and one invited guest on separate internet connections, with shared discoveries and no duplicate pickups or sales. The session model supports additional guests up to a host-configured capacity, tested after the core release. |
| Usability | Remappable keyboard/mouse controls, scalable text, hazard cues beyond color alone, and skippable or reduced-motion wormhole effects. |

Artifact families begin with minerals, biological specimens, and manufactured relics. NPC dealers at the home anchor buy and sell individual instances with fixed spreads that prevent profitable immediate resale. Supplies, travel, and equipment are credit sinks; access upgrades are permanent. Higher tiers increase reward ranges and recovery demands, without making every common find outperform every lower-tier artifact.

The proposed emergency rule returns a player with exhausted suit reserves to the ship and leaves carried artifacts in a recoverable field cache. Banked finds remain safe. Provide a basic suit, a return route, and a zero-cost starter expedition or recovery contract so failure cannot strand a campaign. Generation failures and cancellations refund uncompleted reservations once; cancellation preserves the specification rather than rerolling it.

The host owns the campaign; guest inventory, shared collection, and credit balance stay in that save. Spending and selling require host permission. Private campaign branches do not exchange inventories. The [technical design](technical-design.md) defines persistence and reconnect details.

## Milestones

| Milestone | Deliverable and exit criterion |
| --- | --- |
| P0: greybox expedition prototype | Runs in parallel with M0a for one to two weeks in `prototypes/`, which nothing in `src/` may reference. One hard-coded landing region, three sites, four artifacts, a cargo limit forcing a choice of two, a return, a sale, and a second destination choice; no generation and no persistence. Three to five players; record whether each can explain a choice that changed their outcome and whether they choose another trip. Findings feed M1; the code is discarded ([decision 0015](decisions/0015-greybox-prototype.md)). |
| M0a: primitive-set foundation | First implementation step: a C# core and CLI generate random, constraint-valid primitive sets from authored templates; allocate IDs; validate, store, reload, and query sets without rerolling; specify cross-package set operations without implementing them. Verify canonical hashes on Linux and Windows, missing/corrupt dependencies, ID boundaries/collisions, bounded failures, interrupted publication, and expansion compatibility. Add a minimal Godot viewer and native packaged smoke tests; tool versions are pinned ([decision 0016](decisions/0016-platform-confirmed-and-toolchain-pinned.md)) and reference machines are recorded. Pass the content gate: a fixed rendered sample is sorted as distinct by reviewers against a pre-recorded threshold, and the automated silhouette comparison finds no near-duplicates ([decision 0002](decisions/0002-primitive-set-content-gate.md)). |
| M0b: world generation and save prototype | Compose worlds and artifacts from stored M0a sets. Reproduce fixed-seed data; validate required planet/life and route constraints; bound failures; resume interrupted generation; preserve old worlds when sets expand. Measure one representative region, including stored authoritative region size against the budget of [decision 0017](decisions/0017-region-extent-cap-and-storage-derivation.md), and persistence on both platforms; compare generated terrain output between the two OSes. |
| M1: solo playable expedition | Complete destination selection, travel, landing, recovery, return, sale, second-tier unlocking, and revisit. Removed items remain removed after reload. Testers can explain a recovery or collection choice that changed their outcome. |
| M2: connected core release | Begins with a mixed-OS host/guest connection on the chosen connectivity path, in both host directions. Two PCs on different networks share a saved destination; simultaneous pickups and retried sales commit once; disconnect/rejoin restores consistent state. Meet the verification criteria below. |
| M3: expansion experiments | Evaluate days-long preparation, continuous distance within tiers, chained travel between discovered systems, more regions/primitives, authoring tools, and larger groups after the core loop and compatibility contract work. |

P0 is disposable. Its exit record precedes the first M0a step that freezes a content format, the set specification record of criterion 1 ([decision 0022](decisions/0022-p0-gates-the-first-frozen-content-format.md)); M0a work that freezes no content format proceeds alongside it. M0 means M0a followed by M0b; world generation must not substitute for the primitive-set foundation. M1 is an internal playable slice. It does not satisfy the original internet-sharing requirement alone; M2 is the proposed core release. The connectivity approach is decided at M1 start, once a generated system can be explored locally from a stored save, with a self-hosted relay and coordination server evaluated first ([decision 0003](decisions/0003-network-topology-and-transport.md)).

Long-mode experiments require resumable work, bounded resources, progress visibility, and continued access to prepared destinations. Closing the game pauses computation. A worker that runs while the game is closed is a separate opt-in feature; computation cannot continue on a powered-off PC. Computation is never padded to lengthen a wait; a tier that generates too quickly receives a larger content budget, not a timer.

## Verification and performance targets

These are proposed targets, not benchmark results. M0 records the reference PC, OS, build, content pack, and seed suite. Measure useful playable content separately from total generation cost.

| Check | Acceptance target |
| --- | --- |
| Native platform support | Build and smoke-test on Linux and Windows. On clean target environments, launch the distributed game and verify graphics/input, primitive-set generation and loading, local saves in the data root of [decision 0018](decisions/0018-data-root-region-encoding-and-save-integrity.md), and hosting/joining without requiring an editor or development SDK. Include a mixed Linux/Windows co-op test and cross-platform save compatibility. |
| Primitive-set foundation | Fixed seed/template/algorithm versions and pinned base pack revisions produce matching canonical set data, including the derived pack identifier, across both OSes and worker counts. Saved accepted definitions reload without regeneration; corrupt/missing dependencies fail explicitly. Test zero/reserved IDs, values above signed-32 range, `uint.MaxValue`, overflow rejection, duplicate allocation, and mixed-pack lookup. Measure packed-ID bytes separately from manifests/assets, plus lookup and set-operation costs. |
| Determinism | Matching authoritative descriptors, recipes, placements, and prices for identical specifications, including the campaign branch and the set of existing appearance hashes, across supported builds, thread counts, and interrupted/resumed runs. Terrain generation output is compared across Linux and Windows at M0b as a measured target; a mismatch triggers fixed-point noise before M1 ([decision 0001](decisions/0001-materialize-authoritative-terrain.md)). The suite runs generation in two separate processes and the banned-API analyzer gates the core build ([decision 0008](decisions/0008-random-stream-derivation.md)). |
| Destination validity | At least 1,000 seeds per initial tier plus invalid/boundary inputs. Every accepted world satisfies the two-planet/life, reachable-site, and return-route rules. Retries terminate; record fallback frequency. Simulate a 50-world campaign and record appearance-collision, substitution, and residual-rejection rates; rejection for exhausted variation must be rare and reported ([decision 0007](decisions/0007-artifact-collision-substitution-and-campaign-salt.md)). |
| Compatibility | Save a world, expand the registry, and revisit visited and unvisited regions; identities, geography, artifacts, and prices match the old world. |
| Durability | Interrupt generation, pickup, recovery, and sale around commit boundaries; reload gives consistent state without duplicate items, lost committed credit, or double refunds. Edit a cached credit total, an artifact value, and a ledger row in a saved campaign: each load derives credits from the ledger and reports the campaign as modified ([decision 0018](decisions/0018-data-root-region-encoding-and-save-integrity.md)). |
| Multiplayer | Test separate internet connections, late join, simultaneous pickup, repeated commands, permissions, version mismatch, packet delay, disconnect, rejoin, coordination-server unavailability with direct connection by address, and host-side hazard authority so that a client-reported reserve value is ignored ([decision 0013](decisions/0013-host-simulates-hazards.md)). Run the command and handshake suite over the in-memory transport from M0a onward. A LAN-only demonstration is insufficient. |
| Responsiveness | Target a ready starter destination within 60 seconds, surface exploration at 30 FPS or better at 1080p, and a visible pause/cancel response within one second on the reference PC. |
| Resource use | Over 20 expeditions, record peak RAM, preparation time, package/cache size, and bytes per region/artifact. Bytes per region confirms the derivation in [decision 0017](decisions/0017-region-extent-cap-and-storage-derivation.md): a maximal 2,048 m region is 3 MiB raw and compresses to at most 1 MiB, or the biome map moves to 4 m cells. Demonstrate safe eviction and low-disk recovery; derive shipping limits from measurements. |
| Artifact variety | Review generated samples for duplicate recipes, near-identical silhouettes, unusable shapes, and biome repetition; the M0a content gate is the first application, using a silhouette comparison from fixed viewpoints. Players distinguish families and explain collection choices. |
| Gameplay/economy | Observe short expeditions, second-tier unlocking, and zero-credit recovery. Faster hardware, idling, seed re-entry, reloads, reconnects, and immediate resale create no extra value. |

A short expedition should offer a full recovery-and-sale loop in approximately 15–30 minutes once controls are familiar. Before expanding scope, observe at least five relevant players completing the solo slice; record their choices, what they found distinctive, and whether they choose another trip. This is formative feedback, not a market-size or retention estimate.

Seed suites provide regression coverage rather than proof for every possible world. Runtime constraints and bounded fallback remain required. Add regression cases for discovered generator and transaction failures.

## Decisions before production commitments

| Decision | How to resolve it |
| --- | --- |
| Long-wait role | Decided: preparation is real generation time ([decision 0004](decisions/0004-preparation-is-real-generation-time.md)). Remaining M3 question: whether very large budgets that take hours or days produce content players value, tested with a resumable long-mode prototype. |
| Pricing and progression | Simulate repeated expeditions, collection-versus-sale choices, reward budgets, and unlock pacing with the tier-base-plus-complexity effort score ([decision 0004](decisions/0004-preparation-is-real-generation-time.md)). Elapsed-time pricing is not retained. |
| Stack validation, OS versions, hardware | Godot 4.7.2 .NET/C# on .NET 10 and SQLite are confirmed and pinned ([decision 0016](decisions/0016-platform-confirmed-and-toolchain-pinned.md)), not yet measured. M0a/M0b validate native packaging, one generated region, and a saved artifact on both OSes; the networking proof runs at M2 start. Confirm initial x86_64/Compatibility scope, supported distributions/Windows versions, graphics requirements, and reference machines before publishing minimum requirements. Revisit the stack only if evidence exposes a material mismatch. |
| Numeric content and suit limits | Units, position encoding, heightfield resolution, and simulation tick are fixed by [decision 0010](decisions/0010-units-coordinates-and-region-bounds.md), and the region cap by [decision 0017](decisions/0017-region-extent-cap-and-storage-derivation.md). Measure generation/storage costs, then tune region counts, artifact counts, effort scores, prices, suit envelopes, and site difficulty. Store numeric tables with explicit versions. |
| Camera and art direction | Use M1 navigation and artifact-distinguishability feedback to settle presentation. |
| Internet hosting | An ENet peer is the initial transport and the core owns replication. The intended topology is a loosely coupled network of hosts with all compute on the host, coordinated by a simple cloud server for directory, invitation, and relay. Decide the connectivity approach at M1 start, evaluating a self-hosted relay and coordination server first; demonstrate mixed-OS play in both host directions across separate networks at M2 start. Document account/service dependencies, costs, and unavailable-service behavior. No service is selected yet; solo play remains offline ([decision 0003](decisions/0003-network-topology-and-transport.md)). |
| Schedule and budget | The project owner supplies team capacity and budget; estimate milestones from prototype results. No delivery date is implied by this document. |

The [assessment](assessment.md) records the evidence, assumptions, and positioning questions that should inform these decisions. The [technology decision register](technology-stack.md#gap-filling-decisions-for-confirmation) lists the added platform and library defaults for user confirmation.
