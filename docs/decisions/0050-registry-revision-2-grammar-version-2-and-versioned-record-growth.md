# 0050. Registry revision 2 derives what contradicted itself, grammar version 2 spaces the orbits, and a versioned record may grow

Date: 2026-09-08. Status: Accepted. Source: the owner's verdict on solar-system iteration 1, recorded 2026-09-08 in [progress](../progress.md#solar-system-iterations). Clears [review finding 47](../review.md#47-nothing-holds-independently-drawn-parameters-of-one-body-physically-consistent). Extends [decision 0049](0049-suit-profile-and-system-description-version-1-and-derivation-rules-as-named-implementations.md) with three more derivation rules and takes the system description to version 2.

## Context

Iteration 1 described twenty-four systems and the owner ruled them neither plausible nor varied. Three faults accounted for it. A body's mass and radius were drawn independently, so its density was whatever the two happened to imply, and the sample held a body four times the density of osmium; a star's spectral class and its luminosity were drawn beside its effective temperature and contradicted it, one star printing as class F at 9,628 K. Every semi-major axis was drawn from one range spanning a twentieth of an astronomical unit to forty, so systems came out scattered, listed in the order the generator attached them rather than in orbit order as [decision 0045](0045-close-views-only-in-the-surface-sky-and-a-derived-system-description.md) requires, and mostly far too cold to land on. And the `basic` vocabulary asked for three planet definitions and two stars, so all 208 bodies of the sample reused three mass-radius pairs: variety came from the set, not from the seed. The verdict named all three for iteration 2.

## Decision

**A versioned record may grow.** A canonical record whose domain label carries its own version or revision may add a field in a later one: the writer emits it only from that version onward, and the reader, which dispatches on the label it opened, expects it only from that version onward. An earlier version's bytes are therefore untouched and its recorded hash still holds. This is what schema-driven encoding is for ([decision 0020](0020-canonical-encoding-and-content-hash.md), [decision 0035](0035-category-registry-record-and-generator-revision-identifiers.md)); it is stated here because registry revision 2 and grammar version 2 are the first records to use it, and because the alternative, a new record kind per change, would multiply formats for no gain.

**Registry revision 2 derives what contradicted itself.** A planet stores a mean density and no radius, and its radius follows from its mass and that density by `derive-radius/1`. A star stores neither a spectral class nor a luminosity, and both follow from its effective temperature and radius by `derive-spectral-class/1` and `derive-luminosity/1`. Each category declares what it no longer stores as a *derived parameter*, a label with the generator revision identifier of the rule that computes it, which is the computed default of [decision 0037](0037-derivation-rules-integer-periods-and-orbit-hierarchy.md) and the pin [decision 0035](0035-category-registry-record-and-generator-revision-identifiers.md) requires. Nothing else changes from revision 1. A quantity that is derived cannot disagree with what it is derived from, which is the whole of finding 47; the same rule applied to the region constants is [decision 0017](0017-region-extent-cap-and-storage-derivation.md).

**A generator revision identifier is a path, not text.** Identifiers of the form `derive-radius/1` contain a separator that the canonical text encoding does not admit, so no registry or manifest could ever have written one; both now write them as canonical paths, as a domain label already was. This was latent in [decision 0042](0042-category-registry-revision-1-first-content-records-generator-and-publish-protocol.md) and unreachable only because no record had listed an identifier yet.

**Grammar version 2 spaces the orbits.** A connector rule may carry a *spacing ratio*, and the child at index `i` of an orbit connector draws its semi-major axis from the band that begins at the rule's own minimum multiplied by that ratio `i` times. The children of one connector therefore come out ordered and geometrically spaced, which is how the bodies of a real system are arranged, instead of scattered over one wide range. Version 2 spaces planets at 1.55 from three tenths of an astronomical unit and moons at 1.8. A rule may carry a ratio only on an orbit connector, only from grammar version 2, and only where the whole progression fits inside its own declared range, all three refused at construction.

**System description version 2.** The listing is sorted by semi-major axis and numbered from the star outwards, so it is in orbit order whatever order the generator attached the bodies in, which decision 0045 requires and which no generator change can be relied on to provide. The star line gains its derived luminosity in solar units and a body line its mean density, because those are what the reader now needs to judge the very things this revision changed. A quantity the registry still stores is read, and one it no longer stores is derived, so a graph of either revision describes.

**The vocabulary is authored for the newest revision.** `generate-set` generates under the newest revision this build supports, and `content/templates/basic/vocabulary.json` is authored for it: three star templates whose temperature bands no longer need a class beside them, three atmospheres, and four body templates that draw a density in the range their type implies, with the request counts raised so a system draws from tens of definitions rather than three. Packs published under revision 1 keep loading, and `compose`, `describe`, and `iterate` select the grammar version the content pins, so iteration 1 still reproduces from its record.

## Consequences

Easier: a body's size, mass, and density agree by construction, and so do a star's class, temperature, radius, and luminosity, so the description can be read for plausibility rather than for arithmetic; systems have the shape of systems; the set is no longer the bottleneck on variety. Harder: two registry revisions and two grammar versions are now retained, and every command must select by what the content pins rather than by what the build prefers; the vocabulary is authored against one revision, so a revision that changes a schema needs it rewritten; and the description's own wording moved again, so its vector moved with it.

Iteration 2 ran on this and its measurements are in [progress](../progress.md#solar-system-iterations): 36 distinct bodies where there were 3, 8 stars where there were 2 and every class agreeing with its temperature, orbits ordered from 0.305 to 9.978 astronomical units, and 51 landing candidates where there were 7. What it did not fix is recorded there for the owner's verdict, chiefly that a moon may still outweigh its planet, that a body's type is unrelated to its temperature, and that an atmosphere is unrelated to the gravity that would have to hold it.

Verified by the Destination validity and Determinism checks in the [development plan](../development-plan.md#verification-and-performance-targets): the published-figure vectors of the three new rules, the frozen hashes of both registry revisions and both grammar versions, and the recorded description vector, on Linux and Windows.
