# 0058. The tier rules are a versioned record a destination pins

Date: 2026-09-10. Status: Accepted. Source: the solar-system storage audit of 2026-09-10, which found that a destination pins everything that decides its verdict except the rules that gave it. Applies the numeric-table rule of [decision 0049](0049-suit-profile-and-system-description-version-1-and-derivation-rules-as-named-implementations.md) to the per-tier rules of [decision 0044](0044-tiered-system-composition-one-star-and-explorability-by-validation.md). It clears no review finding: the audit deliberately raised none, because the work was already in flight when it read the code.

## Context

A stored destination is a claim: these two lever values name this system, and this system satisfies this tier. [Decision 0053](0053-a-destination-is-a-stored-record-addressed-by-its-levers.md) makes the claim addressable by pinning everything the answer depends on — the generator version, the category registry revision and hash, the grammar version and hash, the source pack and manifest hash, the description version, and the suit profile's version and hash. The suit profile is pinned by version *and* hash for one stated reason: its limits decide the landing candidates a tier's rules count, so a retuned limit must be a new version and must show in what it produced ([decision 0049](0049-suit-profile-and-system-description-version-1-and-derivation-rules-as-named-implementations.md)).

The rules that read those counts had no such record. `TierRules` held the counts as literals — exactly one explorable planet and no life at the starter tier, at least two and at least one life-bearing body above it, from classes F, G, K, and M — with no version, no hash, and no domain label. A destination therefore pinned the tier's *enum value* and nothing about what that tier demanded, so a stored "pass" could not be re-checked against the rules that produced it, and [decision 0044](0044-tiered-system-composition-one-star-and-explorability-by-validation.md)'s numbers could be retuned without any record moving. Decision 0044 already promises more tuning of exactly these numbers: it states that each tier's count of non-explorable planets and its count of regions per explorable planet come from the numeric tables, and [progress](../progress.md#verification-and-performance-targets) records those as untuned. The first of them to be tuned would silently reinterpret every destination published before it.

The same argument that made the suit profile a record therefore applies one input over, and it is the last of the three that decide a verdict to be held as literals. The fourth, the minimum landable radius, is [review finding 51](../review.md#51-the-minimum-landable-radius-decides-a-verdict-from-a-code-constant) and this record does not clear it.

## Decision

**The tier rules are a numeric table under [decision 0049](0049-suit-profile-and-system-description-version-1-and-derivation-rules-as-named-implementations.md).** The tier profile is one canonical record with domain label `tier-profile/1`, encoded under [decision 0020](0020-canonical-encoding-and-content-hash.md) and identified exactly by its content hash, defined in code as version 1 and moving to `content/` on the trigger that moves the category registry and the composition grammar ([decision 0035](0035-category-registry-record-and-generator-revision-identifiers.md), [decision 0048](0048-composition-grammar-version-1-graph-records-and-graph-publication.md)). It is another frozen content format before the P0 exit record, as [decision 0055](0055-definition-level-tags-and-tagged-references.md) and its predecessors were, and is counted as one under [decision 0022](0022-p0-gates-the-first-frozen-content-format.md).

**What it holds is every value a tuning would move, one demand per tier in tier order:** a minimum and a maximum count of explorable planets, a minimum and a maximum count of life-bearing bodies, and the spectral classes a tier that requires life may draw its star from. A maximum of `uint.MaxValue` means no upper bound. A profile is refused at version zero, when it does not carry exactly one demand per tier the build knows, and when any maximum falls below its minimum; decoding refuses trailing bytes and a non-canonical encoding, as every canonical record does.

**What stays implementation is the shape of the rules, named by the version.** That a tier counts planets and not moons ([decision 0049](0049-suit-profile-and-system-description-version-1-and-derivation-rules-as-named-implementations.md)), that life follows the presence of a life primitive, and that the star-class rule applies only where the demand requires life, are the version's meaning, exactly as a derivation rule's arithmetic is its identifier's meaning ([decision 0037](0037-derivation-rules-integer-periods-and-orbit-hierarchy.md)). Changing any of them is `tier-profile/2`, not an edit.

**Version 1 is what decision 0044 states and what iterations 1 to 4 were judged against.** The starter tier demands exactly one explorable planet and no life, and draws its star from any class. The tier above it demands at least two explorable planets, at least one life-bearing body, and a star of class F, G, K, or M. No number changes here; this record gives the numbers already in force a version and a hash.

**A destination pins the profile its verdict came from.** The destination specification carries the tier profile's version and hash after the suit profile's pair, refusing a zero version or an unset hash as it does for the suit. The pack identifier of a destination is the hash of that specification ([decision 0053](0053-a-destination-is-a-stored-record-addressed-by-its-levers.md)), so a destination's identity now depends on the tier profile, and a tuned profile gives the same levers a different destination rather than the same one with a different meaning.

**An iteration row pins it too.** An iteration is judged by every system's verdict against its tier's rules ([decision 0047](0047-usable-storage-and-structure-generation-then-solar-system-iterations.md)), so the tier profile's version and hash join the registry revision, vocabulary hash, grammar version, generator, description version, suit profile, and, for a row read from pictures, `derive-surface-raster`'s version ([decision 0056](0056-surface-raster-rule-and-the-boundary-of-stream-derivation.md)), both in a row of [progress](../progress.md#solar-system-iterations) and in the `pins` block the `iterate` command prints.

## Consequences

**Nothing about content moves; only destination identity does.** No set, graph, definition, manifest, registry, grammar, or vocabulary hash changes, and the system description's wording and version are untouched, so its recorded fixed-seed vector does not move either. What moves is the destination specification's layout and therefore every destination pack identifier: over set `aba608e6` the starter tier at seed 21 now names pack `15114b2f741cae73f436693dd9670ad9` where it named `ab0596fbb6150b2da811ccfc1f46f09b`, on the same winning attempt and the same composition seed, over the same graph `8fdad8bb`. Identity moved, content did not.

**The 48 destinations published under the old specification stay loadable by their pack identifier and are no longer what their levers name.** That is the cost of the fix, and it is acceptable now precisely because a destination published during the iterations is disposable output rather than a world anyone holds ([decision 0047](0047-usable-storage-and-structure-generation-then-solar-system-iterations.md)); the same change after the core release would reissue worlds players own. Taking it now rather than at the first tuning of decision 0044's counts is the whole reason for doing it before that tuning.

Easier: the numbers a verdict rests on are one record instead of literals in three places, so tuning a tier is a version bump that shows in everything it produced, and the untuned per-tier planet and region counts decision 0044 defers have a home to arrive in rather than a class to be edited. A tuned profile is demonstrably both a different hash and a different verdict on the same system. Harder: one more version to pin beside the registry revision, the grammar version, the generator version, the description version and the suit profile, and one more record whose version an iteration row must carry.

**What must be verified.** The Destination validity and Determinism checks of the [development plan](../development-plan.md#verification-and-performance-targets), on the evidence [progress](../progress.md#verification-and-performance-targets) records: that version 1 is decision 0044's numbers and round-trips through its encoding; that a tuned profile is both a different hash and a different verdict on the same system; that a destination pins the profile its verdict came from and a different profile gives a different pack; and that a profile not covering this build's tiers, at version zero, or with an inverted range is refused. Outstanding at the time of writing: the `iterate` command's `pins` block prints the suit profile and not the tier profile, so the clause above that an iteration row pins it is not yet held by the harness that prints one.

**What this does not fix.** [Review finding 51](../review.md#51-the-minimum-landable-radius-decides-a-verdict-from-a-code-constant) is the same fault one input over: the minimum landable radius of [decision 0041](0041-planet-fields-tangent-regions-minimum-radius-and-far-field.md) is a code constant outside every record, and it refuses a body before the suit profile is consulted. That finding stays open and is not cleared here. Nor does this record make the second tier composable: no registry revision defines a life category, so the life demand version 1 states cannot be met by any system ([review finding 52](../review.md#52-life-is-a-structural-absence-so-the-second-tier-can-never-be-composed)).
