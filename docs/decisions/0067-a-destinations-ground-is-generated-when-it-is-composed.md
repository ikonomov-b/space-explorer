# 0067. A destination's ground is generated when it is composed, because storing all of it fits

Date: 2026-09-11. Status: Accepted. Source: the owner's rule of 2026-09-11 — "storing explored areas in more detail is only relevant if storing everything would take too much space" — put to him with the measurement that says it does not, and his acceptance of generating every region when the destination is composed ("go ahead with the suggested way"). He added one consideration the same day, carried in clause 5: "there is a high probability the primitive set to be extended a lot". Refines clauses 1 and 3 of [decision 0066](0066-generation-writes-to-the-database-and-the-view-only-reads.md) and supersedes no record: 0066 clause 1 already says the *generation step* produces a payload and the view only loads one, and this fixes when that step runs.

## Context

[Decision 0066](0066-generation-writes-to-the-database-and-the-view-only-reads.md) put the ground in the database and made the view a reader, and it was built with the generation triggered by the first view of a region — the view asking the store for ground, and the store generating where none was held. That is a fallback dressed as a rule: 0066 clause 1 says a view "calls no generation rule", and a view that causes one to run on the turn it draws has only moved the call one frame away.

The reason to trigger on first view would be that generating everything costs too much to keep, and the owner stated the test for that directly: selective storage earns its complexity only if storing everything would take too much space. The measurement, taken across the 57 graphs this workstation holds, says it would not. A destination carries 0 to 11 regions, median 4 and mean 4.6. At the packing [decision 0066](0066-generation-writes-to-the-database-and-the-view-only-reads.md) measured — 0.38 to 0.76 MiB a region — a mean destination's whole ground is 1.8 to 3.5 MiB and the largest is 8.4 MiB, and every region of every destination stored here is 100 to 201 MiB against the 300 MiB [decision 0017](0017-region-extent-cap-and-storage-derivation.md) budgets a 300-region campaign. Generating all of one destination's regions costs 4.6 regions at 53 to 322 ms, a second or two, inside a travel wait [decision 0004](0004-preparation-is-real-generation-time.md) already makes real generation time rather than a loading screen.

## Decision

**1. Composition generates and publishes every region's ground, before the destination is indexed (open; the owner's choice).** When a destination is composed, each region node of its graph has its heightfield produced and written as a `region-payload/1` record, and only then is the destination record published. A stored destination is therefore never one whose ground is missing: it is complete or it is absent, and exploring one reads records written before exploring began.

**2. The threshold that would change this is named, so a later reader knows what to re-measure (follows).** Generating everything is right while everything fits. What would make it wrong is a body offering more surface than the budget holds, and the quantity to watch is **regions per landable body**, not destinations per campaign: the grammar has placed exactly one region per admitted body since version 6, and the whole surface of a body at the minimum landable radius is some 823,500 regions, so the gap between what is generated and what could be is five orders of magnitude and nothing has yet moved into it. A campaign count moves slowly and predictably; a rung that places many regions or chunks on one body is what would break the budget, and this clause is what says to measure again rather than to assume.

**3. First view stays as the fallback, and stops being the trigger (follows).** The store still generates where it is asked for ground it does not hold, because a graph published before this record carries none and a field whose parameters have moved needs new ground. What changes is that in the ordinary case there is nothing left for it to do. The fallback is also what clause 2's threshold would promote back to the rule.

**4. What this does not change (follows).** The record format, the storage policy of registry revision 8, the packing, the index, and the view's read-only path are all [decision 0066](0066-generation-writes-to-the-database-and-the-view-only-reads.md)'s and are untouched. No registry revision or grammar version is needed for this: when a payload is produced is not something a category declares.

**5. What a much larger primitive set does and does not touch (follows, on the owner's statement).** The owner expects the set to grow substantially, and the store's costs do not all scale with it, so what does is worth separating from what does not. A definition averages 174 bytes and a manifest entry about 41, so ten thousand definitions are some 1.7 MB of content and 0.4 MB of manifest — but 41 MB of 4 KiB filesystem blocks, one record to a file, and 10,001 hash verifications per load. Today's 118 records load and decode in under 0.08 s, so ten thousand is seconds at worst; it is to be measured at the first vocabulary past a thousand definitions rather than assumed, and the Resource-use row of [progress](../progress.md#verification-and-performance-targets) is where the measurement goes.

Untouched by set size: a graph's bytes, since a definition reference is a varint of one byte below 128 and two below 16,384; a payload, which hangs off the graph pack and describes ground rather than content; and a destination record, which pins its set by pack identifier and manifest hash whatever that set holds. Made dearer: each registry revision re-mints the whole set, at 1.7 MB a revision rather than the 25 KiB [decision 0063](0063-relief-by-terrain-heightfield-registry-revision-6-and-grammar-version-7.md) called free, and the deletion sweep of [decision 0040](0040-reference-index-and-deletion-sweep.md) has correspondingly more to reclaim. The per-record layout stays until a measurement says otherwise, which is [decision 0024](0024-tests-comments-index-and-scaffolding-trimmed.md)'s rule rather than an opinion about packing.

## Consequences

Easier: the view's claim to call no generation rule becomes true of the ordinary path rather than of the interface only; the cost of a destination's ground is paid inside the wait that exists to be paid in, and is one figure a reader can see rather than a stutter spread across a session; and a destination is a complete thing on disk, which is what "available for multiple visiting" asks for.

Harder: composing a destination now costs a second or two and some megabytes where it cost milliseconds and kilobytes, so the iteration sweeps that compose two dozen destinations feel it; ground nobody ever visits is generated and kept, which is the waste this record accepts deliberately because measuring says it is affordable; and the deletion sweep of [decision 0040](0040-reference-index-and-deletion-sweep.md), already named as necessary rather than tidy ([storage inspection finding 3](../inspection-storage.md#3-two-of-the-five-index-tables-are-written-and-never-read)), now has megabytes rather than kilobytes to reclaim per superseded destination.

**What must be verified**, on the evidence [progress](../progress.md#surface-iterations) records: a freshly composed destination having a payload for every region of its graph before anything is drawn; a view of it reporting that it loaded rather than generated; the cost of composing one destination measured; and the data root's region-ground figure after a sweep of the sample, against decision 0017's budget.
