# 0049. Suit profile and system description version 1, and derivation rules as named implementations

Date: 2026-09-08. Status: Accepted. Source: [decision 0047](0047-usable-storage-and-structure-generation-then-solar-system-iterations.md) step two, with the suit profile's limits chosen by the owner on 2026-09-08 from the options in [review finding 46](../review.md#46-the-suit-profile-and-the-numeric-tables-have-no-home-no-version-and-no-values). Implements the describe command of [decision 0045](0045-close-views-only-in-the-surface-sky-and-a-derived-system-description.md) and the first two rules of [decision 0037](0037-derivation-rules-integer-periods-and-orbit-hierarchy.md). Clears [review finding 46](../review.md#46-the-suit-profile-and-the-numeric-tables-have-no-home-no-version-and-no-values) and raises [finding 47](../review.md#47-nothing-holds-independently-drawn-parameters-of-one-body-physically-consistent).

## Context

Decision 0047 makes the describe command the first deliverable of step two, because it is the criterion the solar-system iterations are judged by, and it fixes what decides the landing-candidate flag until regions exist: a reference radius of at least 524,288 m ([decision 0041](0041-planet-fields-tangent-regions-minimum-radius-and-far-field.md)) and derived surface gravity, surface pressure, and equilibrium temperature inside the suit profile's envelope. Three things were missing. The suit profile has never had a number: [decision 0044](0044-tiered-system-composition-one-star-and-explorability-by-validation.md) makes explorability a validation outcome against "the suit profile's numeric limits", the development plan calls repeatedly for versioned numeric tables, and no document or record holds one. The derivation rules of decision 0037 exist as identifiers and prose, with no implementation and no vectors. And decision 0037 makes a derivation rule a primitive of a `derivation rule` category, which category registry revision 1 does not define.

## Decision

**Numeric tables are canonical records, defined in code until an authoring tool needs them.** A numeric table is one canonical record identified by its content hash, with a version in its domain label. The suit profile is the first: domain `suit-profile/1`, version 1 defined in code and frozen by its recorded hash, its source form moving to `content/` when an authoring tool exists, exactly as [decision 0035](0035-category-registry-record-and-generator-revision-identifiers.md) says of the category registry and [decision 0048](0048-composition-grammar-version-1-graph-records-and-graph-publication.md) of the composition grammar. Every artefact a table decides pins the version and the hash, so a retuned limit is a new version and is visible in what it produced. This keeps measurement out of hashed definitions for the same reason [decision 0034](0034-storage-policy-pinned-by-manifests.md) keeps the storage policy out of one.

**Suit profile version 1.** Surface gravity from a tenth of standard gravity to one and a half of it, surface pressure up to two megapascals, and equilibrium temperature from 120 K to 400 K. The limits state what the suit is built for rather than what a person survives unaided: a pressure suit already handles vacuum and cold, so what bounds a world is gravity that can be walked and carried in, pressure the suit resists, and the span its life support holds. Vacuum is admitted, so pressure has no lower limit. The values are untuned and the iterations revise them; the owner accepted that a starter system will therefore often show more than the one explorable planet decision 0044 requires, and that the correction belongs in generation rather than in a narrower suit.

**Derivation rules are named implementations before the registry names a category.** `derive-surface-gravity/1` and `derive-equilibrium-temperature/1` exist as retained integer implementations with recorded vectors, identified by the generator revision identifiers of decision 0035 and retained under its rule. No floating point takes part, every division rounds to nearest, and the intermediates are 128-bit so nothing overflows inside registry revision 1's parameter ranges. Their vectors are the solar system's own bodies: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, and the Moon, checked against the published surface gravities and black-body temperatures of the NASA planetary fact sheets, so no vector rests on this repository's code alone. A `derivation rule` category, and with it the stored-or-computed declaration decision 0037 describes, arrives when a graph must *reference* a rule rather than a tool applying one to derive a description; that is a registry revision and it is not this one.

**System description version 1.** The description states the star's class, temperature, mass, and radius, then every body in orbit order with its number, role, type, distance from the star, mass, radius, derived surface gravity, its atmosphere primitive's surface pressure and composition, derived equilibrium temperature, and either the landing-candidate flag or the limits that refused it, then the totals decision 0045 requires. It carries the derived numbers because a flag cannot be judged plausible without them, which is the description's stated purpose. A body's distance from the star is the semi-major axis of the orbit hanging from the star on its ancestry, so a moon shares its planet's distance and a planet on a barycentre shares the barycentre's, which is the two-body hierarchy of decision 0037. The text is never stored; its content hash is the fixed-seed vector decision 0045 asks for, and the wording is part of the version.

**A moon is not counted as an explorable planet.** Decision 0044 counts moons separately from planets, so only a body hanging from the star or from a barycentre counts towards a tier's explorable-planet rule, however landable a moon is. Registry revision 1 has no moon category, so the role comes from the connector a body hangs on, not from its category.

**The iteration harness.** `iterate` composes one system per seed and describes it without publishing anything, because systems generated in an iteration are disposable and are never kept destinations (decision 0047); it prints what it pins, every description, each system's tier verdict, and the totals and refusal counts of the whole sample. `describe` prints the same description from a published graph, which is what the M1 arrival screen will show.

## Consequences

Easier: the generator has a human-readable criterion before anything is rendered, so the owner can judge system variety from a terminal; the first numeric table has a home, a version, and a hash, and the next ones follow it; two physical relationships are now anchored to published measurements on both operating systems; a tier verdict is mechanical and names the rule it broke. Harder: the description's wording is frozen by its vector, so improving a column is a version change; the suit profile is one more version to pin beside the registry revision, the grammar version, and the generator version; and the landing-candidate flag is provisional until regions exist, when the validation outcome of decision 0044 replaces it under a new description version.

Iteration 1 ran on this machinery the same day and its measurements are in [progress](../progress.md#solar-system-iterations). They raise [finding 47](../review.md#47-nothing-holds-independently-drawn-parameters-of-one-body-physically-consistent): a body's mass, radius, and albedo, and a star's class, temperature, radius, and luminosity, are drawn independently, so the sample contains a body of four times the density of osmium and a star labelled a class its temperature contradicts. That is a registry-revision question, and it waits on the owner's verdict on the iteration rather than being settled here.

Verified by the Destination validity and Determinism checks in the [development plan](../development-plan.md#verification-and-performance-targets): the recorded description vector on Linux and Windows, the published-figure vectors of the two rules, and the reviewed sample of descriptions that the iterations begin.
