# Space Explorer: implementation progress

Updated 2026-09-08. The single source for where the work stands, so the other documents state status once and link here ([decision 0011](decisions/0011-requirements-single-source.md) applies the same rule to platform policy).

It opens with the record of every step that has passed, then tracks the [development plan](development-plan.md) exactly: the same milestones in the same order, the plan's M0a exit criteria in the order the plan states them, the solar-system iterations the plan's M0b row names, the plan's twelve verification checks under their own names, and the plan's seven open production decisions. Nothing is added to the plan's process and nothing is dropped from it; rows the plan does not contain appear only in the section labelled as such.

A step is recorded as passed only with the evidence that closed it, never on the strength of having been written. Status words mean one thing throughout: **Done** is implemented and checked by the evidence named; **Partial** states exactly which part holds; **Not started** means no code or record exists. "Passes on Linux and Windows" always means both were run, never that one was run and the other inferred.

Local results are from a Debian 13 x86_64 workstation. No performance target has been measured.

What iteration 1 showed, in the order it matters for the verdict.

1. **Variety comes from the set, not from the seed.** An instance names a definition of the pinned set, and the `basic` vocabulary asks for three planet definitions and two star definitions, so all 208 bodies of the sample reused three mass-radius pairs and all 24 systems reused two stars. Seeds vary the structure, the orbits, and therefore the temperatures; they cannot vary a body's mass or a star's class. Raising the request counts is a one-line change to the authored vocabulary.
2. **A body's parameters contradict each other**, which is [finding 47](review.md#47-nothing-holds-independently-drawn-parameters-of-one-body-physically-consistent) and the one thing tuning cannot fix: of the three planet definitions, one is 3.12 Earth masses in a 1,380 km radius, four times the density of osmium, and reads as 653 m/s^2; both stars carry a spectral class their effective temperature contradicts, one printed as class F at 9,628 K; and both disagree with their own luminosity by more than an order of magnitude.
3. **Orbits are drawn uniformly between a twentieth of an astronomical unit and forty**, so the sample spans 0.83 to 39.6 AU and equilibrium temperature refuses more bodies than every other limit together; real systems are spaced geometrically, and this is the generator's problem, of a kind with [finding 47](review.md#47-nothing-holds-independently-drawn-parameters-of-one-body-physically-consistent).
4. **The description lists bodies in the order the generator attached them, which is not orbit order.** [Decision 0045](decisions/0045-close-views-only-in-the-surface-sky-and-a-derived-system-description.md) requires each planet "in orbit order"; only 2 of the sample's 20 multi-planet systems come out in ascending distance, which is what chance gives. Sorting the listing by semi-major axis satisfies that decision whatever the generator draws, and is a description-version change independent of the spacing above.
5. **A moon can outweigh its planet**, because a moon is a planet on a planet's orbit connector under both registry revisions and nothing relates the two masses.
6. **Two of the owner's three reasons for a planet to be unexplorable cannot occur.** The `rocky-planet` template admits `rocky`, `icy`, and `ocean`, so no gas giant is reachable although the registry has the body type, and nothing in registry revision 1 represents a volcanic body at all ([decision 0044](decisions/0044-tiered-system-composition-one-star-and-explorability-by-validation.md)).
7. **Nothing retries at the system level.** A system that fails its tier's rule is simply reported; the world-level retry and fallback of M0a exit criterion 8 is M0b work, and until it exists the starter tier's "exactly one explorable planet" has to come from constrained generation rather than from rejection.


What iteration 2 showed, for its verdict. The three faults iteration 1 named are gone: no body's density contradicts its size, no star's class contradicts its temperature, the orbits are ordered and spaced from 0.305 to 9.978 AU, and the sample draws on 36 distinct bodies and 8 stars rather than 3 and 2. What remains is of one kind, relationships *between* bodies rather than inside one, which is where finding 47's argument now points.

1. **A moon may outweigh its planet.** Fifteen of the sample's moons are gas giants, one of 519 Earth masses orbiting a planet of 0.21. Registry revision 1 and 2 both model a moon as a planet on a planet's orbit connector, and nothing relates the two masses.
2. **A body's type is unrelated to its temperature.** Icy bodies appear at 0.577 AU and 282 K, where ice would not survive; the four body templates draw a density band each and the orbit band is drawn independently of them.
3. **An atmosphere is unrelated to the gravity that would hold it.** A dense hydrogen-helium atmosphere sits on a 0.21 Earth-mass body whose escape velocity could not retain it. [Decision 0037](decisions/0037-derivation-rules-integer-periods-and-orbit-hierarchy.md) names `derive-escape-velocity` and `derive-atmosphere-retention` for exactly this and neither is implemented.
4. **Seventeen of twenty-four systems still miss the starter tier's rule of exactly one explorable planet**, most of them now by having several rather than none: ten systems have no landing candidate at all and the rest have between two and seven. Nothing retries or constrains at the system level, which remains M0b work under M0a exit criterion 8.
5. **Equilibrium temperatures reach 1,459 K** on inner bodies of hot stars, which is physically right for the orbit but leaves the hot end of the range doing little for the game.


What iteration 3 showed, for its verdict. The three relationships iteration 2 named are holding: no moon outweighs its planet, every body type sits in a temperature range that suits it, and no system is uniformly frozen or scorched. Two of iteration 2's observations stand unchanged, and the sample adds one.

1. **An atmosphere is still unrelated to the gravity that would hold it**, which iteration 3 did not attempt. A rocky body of 0.01 Earth masses at 1.39 m/s^2 carries 16 bar of hydrogen and helium, which it could not retain. Pressure is now the commonest refusal, 62 of 158. [Decision 0037](decisions/0037-derivation-rules-integer-periods-and-orbit-hierarchy.md) names `derive-escape-velocity` and `derive-atmosphere-retention` for this; the tag mechanism cannot express it, because an atmosphere is a leaf category with no connector of its own to declare on, so either those rules or a registry revision that gives it one is the way in.
2. **The starter tier's rule is met by 5 systems of 24, and by fewer than before for the reason the work succeeded**: 72 bodies are now landing candidates against 51, so more systems have several explorable planets than have exactly one. Seven have none and twelve have two or three. Nothing constrains or retries at the system level; that is the world-level retry of M0a exit criterion 8, still M0b work, and it is now the largest single thing between the generator and a valid starter destination.
3. **A body's albedo is drawn independently of its type and its zone**, so an icy body can be dark and a rocky one brighter than snow. It is the same shape of fault as the ones iteration 2 fixed, one level down: albedo is a stored parameter that could be derived from, or constrained by, the type it belongs to.
