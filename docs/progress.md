# Space Explorer: implementation progress

Updated 2026-09-11. The single source for where the work stands, so the other documents state status once and link here ([decision 0011](decisions/0011-requirements-single-source.md) applies the same rule to platform policy).

It opens with the record of every step that has passed, then tracks the [development plan](development-plan.md) exactly: the same milestones in the same order, the plan's M0a exit criteria in the order the plan states them, the solar-system iterations the plan's M0b row names, the plan's twelve verification checks under their own names, and the plan's seven open production decisions. Nothing is added to the plan's process and nothing is dropped from it; rows the plan does not contain appear only in the section labelled as such.

A step is recorded as passed only with the evidence that closed it, never on the strength of having been written. Status words mean one thing throughout: **Done** is implemented and checked by the evidence named; **Partial** states exactly which part holds; **Not started** means no code or record exists. "Passes on Linux and Windows" always means both were run, never that one was run and the other inferred.

Local results are from a Debian 13 x86_64 workstation. No performance target has been measured.

2. **A body's parameters contradict each other**, which is [finding 47](review.md#47-nothing-holds-independently-drawn-parameters-of-one-body-physically-consistent) and the one thing tuning cannot fix: of the three planet definitions, one is 3.12 Earth masses in a 1,380 km radius, four times the density of osmium, and reads as 653 m/s^2; both stars carry a spectral class their effective temperature contradicts, one printed as class F at 9,628 K; and both disagree with their own luminosity by more than an order of magnitude.
3. **Orbits are drawn uniformly between a twentieth of an astronomical unit and forty**, so the sample spans 0.83 to 39.6 AU and equilibrium temperature refuses more bodies than every other limit together; real systems are spaced geometrically, and this is the generator's problem, of a kind with [finding 47](review.md#47-nothing-holds-independently-drawn-parameters-of-one-body-physically-consistent).
4. **The description lists bodies in the order the generator attached them, which is not orbit order.** [Decision 0045](decisions/0045-close-views-only-in-the-surface-sky-and-a-derived-system-description.md) requires each planet "in orbit order"; only 2 of the sample's 20 multi-planet systems come out in ascending distance, which is what chance gives. Sorting the listing by semi-major axis satisfies that decision whatever the generator draws, and is a description-version change independent of the spacing above.
5. **A moon can outweigh its planet**, because a moon is a planet on a planet's orbit connector under both registry revisions and nothing relates the two masses.
6. **Two of the owner's three reasons for a planet to be unexplorable cannot occur.** The `rocky-planet` template admits `rocky`, `icy`, and `ocean`, so no gas giant is reachable although the registry has the body type, and nothing in registry revision 1 represents a volcanic body at all ([decision 0044](decisions/0044-tiered-system-composition-one-star-and-explorability-by-validation.md)).
7. **Nothing retries at the system level.** A system that fails its tier's rule is simply reported; the world-level retry and fallback of M0a exit criterion 8 is M0b work, and until it exists the starter tier's "exactly one explorable planet" has to come from constrained generation rather than from rejection.


What iteration 2 showed, for its verdict. The three faults iteration 1 named are gone: no body's density contradicts its size, no star's class contradicts its temperature, the orbits are ordered and spaced from 0.305 to 9.978 AU, and the sample draws on 36 distinct bodies and 8 stars rather than 3 and 2. What remains is of one kind, relationships *between* bodies rather than inside one, which is where finding 47's argument now points.

1. **A moon may outweigh its planet.** Fifteen of the sample's moons are gas giants, one of 519 Earth masses orbiting a planet of 0.21. Registry revision 1 and 2 both model a moon as a planet on a planet's orbit connector, and nothing relates the two masses.
2. **A body's type is unrelated to its temperature.** Icy bodies appear at 0.577 AU and 282 K, where ice would not survive; the four body templates draw a density band each and the orbit band is drawn independently of them.
3. **An atmosphere is unrelated to the gravity that would hold it.** A dense hydrogen-helium atmosphere sits on a 0.21 Earth-mass body whose escape velocity could not retain it. [Decision 0037](decisions/0037-derivation-rules-integer-periods-and-orbit-hierarchy.md) names `derive-escape-velocity` and `derive-atmosphere-retention` for exactly this and neither is implemented.
4. **Seventeen of twenty-four systems still miss the starter tier's rule of exactly one explorable planet**, most of them now by having several rather than none: ten systems have no landing candidate at all and the rest have between two and seven. Nothing retries or constrains at the system level, which remains M0b work under M0a exit criterion 8.
5. **Equilibrium temperatures reach 1,459 K** on inner bodies of hot stars, which is physically right for the orbit but leaves the hot end of the range doing little for the game.

A residual is one height less the planar prediction of three others, so it spans four int16 values — ±131,070, and ±65,536 under the 1,024 m amplitude cap — which is eighteen bits zig-zagged either way. The 640 MiB of [decision 0068](decisions/0068-campaign-storage-budget-re-derived-from-the-payload-ceiling.md) is therefore a ceiling over anything the generator can draw and not over anything the format can hold, and a payload wider than seventeen bits is a finding against the generator or the amplitude cap rather than a rounding.

The rule that record settles is unchanged — storing everything still fits, so selective storage is still unwarranted — but the headroom is a seventh of the budget rather than a third to a half, and a reader deciding anything on the margin should use these figures and not that record's. **From the same day the ground is produced when a destination is composed rather than when a region is first opened** ([decision 0067](decisions/0067-a-destinations-ground-is-generated-when-it-is-composed.md)), on the owner's rule that storing explored areas selectively is only worth it if storing everything would take too much space — and the measurement says it would not: every region of every destination this workstation holds is 100 to 201 MiB against the 300 MiB [decision 0017](decisions/0017-region-extent-cap-and-storage-derivation.md) budgets a campaign, and a destination's mean 4.6 regions cost a second or two to produce, inside a travel wait [decision 0004](decisions/0004-preparation-is-real-generation-time.md) already makes real. The ground is published before the destination record is indexed, so a stored destination is complete or absent rather than one whose ground is missing. Composed into a clean data root, seed 1 reports `ground 2 region(s), 2 generated, 1.91 MiB written` before its pack is named, and the first view of it that has ever happened reports `loaded from the data root`. Generating on first view stays as the fallback, for graphs published before the record and for a field whose parameters have moved.

The record is compacted by arithmetic this repository owns rather than by a library, because a content-addressed store may not let a third party's output decide its hashes: each height is predicted from its left and upper neighbours less the corner between them, and the residual is packed at the bit width its row needs. Measured on the sample's own content, flat ocean ground stores at 19% of its raw samples, smooth ground at 26%, and ridged rock at 38% — and every one of those beats a general-purpose deflate of the same samples. The worst case the format admits is about 106% of raw, on noise the authored vocabulary cannot draw.

**The justification is not speed, and the measurement is what says so.** A maximal region regenerates in 53 ms warm and 322 ms on a cold process's first call, so reading a stored megabyte back is not obviously the cheaper path. What storing buys is that a view which generates is a second implementation of the world and can draw what is not stored, which [decision 0031](decisions/0031-primitive-complete-composition-and-storage.md) forbids of the content-gate viewer; and that a visited surface survives a change to the rule that made it, where a regenerating view would silently redraw every place already seen. `terrain-heightfield` has already gone to a second version once.

What cycle four showed, for its verdict. The ground is made of something now: a planet carries a palette, a region derives 64 patches from its own stream, and each cell takes the nearest patch whose biome admits its elevation, so the material changes across a region along boundaries that wander with the patches rather than following a contour. The owner read it at the window and accepted the relief.

The reading found one fault, and it was the content's rather than the rung's. **A body whose coarsest relief wavelength stands far above the region cap reads as a flat plane whatever its amplitude claims.** The ocean body he walked first drew 7,820 m against a 2,048 m region and produced 13.9 m of relief across two kilometres on a stored amplitude of 124 m — a region sees a fraction of one wave at that scale and nothing about it varies. Every body's coarsest wavelength now sits at or inside [decision 0017](decisions/0017-region-extent-cap-and-storage-derivation.md)'s cap, and an ocean world gets the relief an island world needs rather than the almost-none it had: the same region carries 116.8 m and the rocky body of seed 1 carries 543.3 m. Neither the rung-2 nor the rung-3 reading caught it, because every body either of them looked at happened to draw a short wavelength — a sample that rotates the body kind would not have caught it either, and what would is a row that prints the drawn wavelength against the extent.

Two faults in the rule itself were found by measuring rather than by reading, before the owner saw it, and both are now tested. A **submerged biome took 62% of dry cells at every datum from none to sixty metres**, because the elevation branch admitted a sea floor on land and its default band is the widest one; and when that was fixed the **fallback for a cell no band admits reintroduced it one level down**. Both now prefer land, and the tests pin the shoreline by deriving the index at a datum above and below the region's own samples.

**Rung 3, the biome layer, is built** on [decision 0070](decisions/0070-a-biome-is-a-derived-set-registry-revision-9-and-grammar-version-10.md), accepted on the owner's word of 2026-09-11. A biome is a derived set under [decision 0038](decisions/0038-derived-instances.md) rather than a field stored per cell, on his ruling that "the derived set is the way": patches with identity and parameters, from which the per-cell classification a renderer needs is derived on load by a named rule, as heights already are from a relief field. The storage arithmetic decided it — a stored per-cell index is a megabyte raw per region and 25 to 200 KB run-length coded, on a region already costing 0.97 MiB against a budget re-derived the same day, and its cost would have varied with how blotchy the content looked; the patch array measures 587 B on a maximal region, which is 0.03% of [decision 0068](decisions/0068-campaign-storage-budget-re-derived-from-the-payload-ceiling.md)'s per-region ceiling and inside the 640 B that record's clause 8 claims. Registry revision 9 carries the `biome-element` category, a planet's palette and its `sea-level` datum; grammar version 10 adds no production, because a biome is referenced and never composed. `region-payload` grows to version 2 under [decision 0050](decisions/0050-registry-revision-2-grammar-version-2-and-versioned-record-growth.md)'s rule, so cycles one to three reproduce unmoved.

The palette is the first `RefList` the parameter schema has ever held in code. The [technical design](technical-design.md#primitive-registry-and-composition)'s closed schema has admitted ordered arrays since before registry revision 1 and no category needed one until a palette did, which is the rule `ParameterKind` states of itself. It is also the first use of decision 0038's derived instances: every category built before this one declares a derived budget of zero, so the machinery and the bounded-array check the [persistence table](technical-design.md#persistence-and-compatibility) promises had never run.

What cycle three's sample shows across the kinds, and one thing about the sample itself. **The sweep's own frames could not have answered the question cycle three asks.** A walk frame is drawn of the destination's first region, and the first region of all twenty-four seeds is a rocky body, so forty-eight frames show forty-eight rocky grounds — of the 120 regions the sample holds, 104 are rock, 10 ocean and 6 ice. The change being judged is the one that makes shape depend on what a body is made of, and the sample as swept shows one kind of body. Six frames were therefore rendered of the ice and ocean regions by name, which is a gap in the harness and not in the content: nothing offers a frame per kind, and the reading had to go looking. Whether the sweep should draw a region per kind rather than the first of each destination was the owner's to say, and he settled it on 2026-09-11: it should, and cycle four's sweep does, rotating rocky, icy and ocean by seed index and falling back to the destination's first region where that kind is absent ([decision 0070](decisions/0070-a-biome-is-a-derived-set-registry-revision-9-and-grammar-version-10.md) clause 12).

Read across the three kinds, the ranges hold. **Ice**, at a ridging of 0.475 and 308 m of amplitude, reads as a snowfield — gentle rounded forms with creases in them, which is what ice that flows and then fractures looks like. **Rock**, at 0.777, carries the crests and the broad valleys between them that the fold was built for. **Ocean**, at 0.020, is flat: 13.9 m of relief across 2,048 m, and the whole region below the reference radius at −22.8 m to −8.9 m. It reads as a sea, and it reads that way for the wrong reason — there is no water, only flat ground wearing a blue material, and the ocean datum that would put a surface on it is named in [decision 0063](decisions/0063-relief-by-terrain-heightfield-registry-revision-6-and-grammar-version-7.md)'s deferral table for rung 3. It is right by accident, which is a thing to know before rung 3 makes it right on purpose.

What cycle three changed besides the ridges, all of it the harness's own and all of it named in the legend, which is [decision 0062](decisions/0062-the-surface-harness-walks-in-an-interactive-session.md) clause 5's rule. The owner read cycle two at the window and asked for four things a reader needs and the harness had not supplied. The **sun is drawn in the sky** and stands low, at 26 degrees, because relief is read from the shadows it casts as much as from its shape, and the harness had lit the ground from a direction a reader could not see; it is still not this system's star, and the legend says so. Its **shadows reach across the whole region** rather than Godot's hundred-metre default, which on a 2,048 m plate had shadowed only what was underfoot. **`shift` surveys at 28 times the walking pace**, so a reader crosses a maximal region in some twenty seconds rather than ten minutes of holding a key — a reader judging a whole region is surveying it, not walking it. And the ground's material was twice wrong in ways the content fixed rather than the code: the near pattern was invisible at a 0.348 m tile, so the body surfaces now tile at 1.5 to 4 m, which is 1.88 m on this sample's body; and rock at a stored roughness of 0.69 sheened under the low sun, so its floor is 0.88, while ice and ocean keep theirs, because ice and water do shine.

What rung 2 showed, for its verdict. Relief answers what rung 1's reading said only relief could: from above, the ground that was one flat colour at 2.3 m a pixel now carries ridges and valleys, and the map frame prints the range it is showing beside its scale bar, so the vertical axis can be measured rather than guessed. On foot the region reads as landscape — the eye rides the ground rather than holding an altitude, so a walk crosses it rather than floating over it.

Two faults were found in the reading, in the order they were found.

1. **The near ground read as a grid rather than as ground**, which is [decision 0061](decisions/0061-surface-iterations-by-an-escalating-ladder-registry-revision-5-and-grammar-version-6.md) clause 3's predicted fault arriving on schedule and worse than at rung 1, because a slope shows a walker more of the tile at once. The material tiles every 0.348 m, and version 1 of the raster rule both left a seam at the tile's edge and carried a motif an eighth of a tile across — the first is the stronger cue and neither is a fault where version 1 was used, since a body drawn far from its own size clamps its repeat to between one and eight. The owner chose a rule version over a vocabulary change or a deferral to rung 3, and [decision 0064](decisions/0064-surface-raster-version-2-tiles-seamlessly-and-finely.md) is `derive-surface-raster/2`: the lattice wraps with the tile and every feature is small enough that no motif survives the repeat. Version 1 is retained with its recorded digest unmoved, and the sample above is drawn under version 2. The distant ground, which the owner said already read well, is unchanged — a minified tile is its own average whichever rule drew it.
2. **The ground is smooth and curvy where the body it sits on is rocky**, which is the fault the owner named in accepting the rung, and it is the relief rule's and not the material's. `terrain-heightfield/1` sums smoothstep-interpolated value noise, which is a construction that makes dunes: every feature is rounded, nothing fractures, and no slope is steeper than its neighbours prepare it to be. Rock is angular — ridges, scarps, talus — and the standard way to get it is a ridged construction, the absolute value of a signed lattice inverted so the creases point up, rather than the smooth sum this version takes. It is a `terrain-heightfield/2` and a rung of its own to read it on, and until one is drawn the ladder's rungs 3 to 7 are being built on ground that is the right height and the wrong shape. Nothing about it is a bug: the amplitude, the wavelength and the octave count all do what the record says, and it is the record's own choice of construction that is being judged.

What the earlier rung's frames showed. A maximal region's ground is 4.19 km² and the material tiles across it at the length its recipe's scale claims: on seed 1's body that is 5,891 tiles, 0.348 m each, 0.27 cm a texel. Reading it live exposed a fault the two static frames alone had not: the ground carried a smooth false gradient corner to corner, in the orthographic frame as much as the walk frame, though a flat plane under one directional light has no reason to shade unevenly. The cause was not the content but the harness — `PrimitiveResources.TextureFor` wrapped the raster without mipmaps, so tiling a 128-pixel image several thousand times across the ground aliased into a gradient no source pixel contains, worst exactly where rung one's own question is asked, at extreme minification. Generating mipmaps before wrapping the texture fixed it, and the sample above is re-rendered under that fix; no pinned hash moved, because the bytes `derive-surface-raster/1` produces are untouched and only the engine's own sampling of them changed.

What the fix leaves is the real question rung one exists to ask, and it is not fully answered: up close the material still reads as visibly repetitive rather than as grain with no repeat visible, which is the fault [decision 0061](decisions/0061-surface-iterations-by-an-escalating-ladder-registry-revision-5-and-grammar-version-6.md)'s own clause 3 named in advance as the one a rung-one failure sends back — the recipe's scale range or `derive-surface-raster/2`. The owner accepted rung one with it named and open rather than tuning before accepting, on the precedent of the solar-system iterations, which the same table above records as accepted with named faults more than once. From above the ground is legible with no false structure once the fix is applied, which is what rung one asked the map frame to show.

Three faults are named, none of them fixed by acceptance. The first, the mipmap aliasing above, is fixed in the commit that carries this reading. The second was found before a frame was drawn and is fixed in the build commit: the anchor gated by radius alone admitted every body the `basic` vocabulary can draw, gas giants included, because the smallest it draws is about 640 km against a 524 km minimum. The third is that four of the six systems put their one region on a body the suit refuses — seed 9's first region is on a moon at `no: temperature` — so a region and a landing candidate are not the same thing, by design and not by accident: a body may have ground a person cannot stand on. Whether the sample should be framed on landable bodies instead is the owner's to say. The repetitive grain named above carries forward as a fourth, open item for whichever rung next touches the material.

Rung 2 is built and accepted, on [decision 0063](decisions/0063-relief-by-terrain-heightfield-registry-revision-6-and-grammar-version-7.md). Its arithmetic finds that a stored cube-sphere relief grid at rung 2's 2 m cells costs 1.57 TiB for one minimum-radius planet, so relief is a recipe a planet instance owns, `terrain-heightfield/1`, evaluated on demand rather than stored: registry revision 6 adds the recipe's three parameters to `planet` and declares `region`'s storage policy as regeneration alone — the first category of any revision that is not `Materialize` — and grammar version 7 carries both over grammar version 6's rules unchanged. No bulk or cube-sphere parameter kind and no payload record class was built for it, because nothing is stored: [primitives part 5](primitives.md#part-5-what-the-structure-does-not-yet-carry)'s named gaps stay open for the mutable-state overlay that will actually need them.

Four vectors hold the rule, no two of them resting on the same thing ([decision 0063](decisions/0063-relief-by-terrain-heightfield-registry-revision-6-and-grammar-version-7.md) clause 13). Two hand cases, of sixteen samples each, were produced by a second implementation written from that record's prose alone and outside this repository, and agree with the code byte for byte; the second sits in the octant where every planet-fixed coordinate is negative, because the first cannot catch a truncating division — at the finest wavelength the position divides exactly, and over half a sphere it does not. The lattice's one hash construction is anchored on SplitMix64's published first output for seed 0, which is what `derive-surface-raster/1` never had. A maximal region's 1,025-by-1,025 field carries a recorded digest, which continuous integration compares on both operating systems. And two properties are tested rather than claimed: no sample leaves the stored amplitude at any roughness, and turning a region's heading a quarter turn permutes the same ground exactly — 66,049 samples of it — which is the statement that the field belongs to the planet and not to the region, and what lets rung 5's far field continue a region instead of inventing a second landscape beside it.

Three of the four mutations that record names turn the suite red: dropping the octave normalisation, seeding the field from the definition rather than the instance, and reading the field in the region's frame rather than the planet's. **The fourth does not, and the record is wrong about it.** Taking a truncating division for a negative lattice coordinate at the 2^16 scale changes nothing observable, because the `>> 16` that follows is an arithmetic shift and floors the cell anyway, so the correction it names is a sub-resolution adjustment. The mutation that does bite is one division further down — truncating the *cell* division instead of letting the shift floor it — and it is caught, by the negative-octant hand case above and by the rotation property. The clause's intent holds and its example does not.

The first generation cost worth timing, which [decision 0063](decisions/0063-relief-by-terrain-heightfield-registry-revision-6-and-grammar-version-7.md) clause 14 asks for: one maximal region is 1,050,625 samples over 10 octaves and takes **53 ms warm and 322 ms on a cold process's first call**, in a release build on the reference workstation, across its cores — the rows being independent and the bytes identical however they are scheduled. **The figure first recorded here, 2.7 seconds single-threaded and 0.46 parallel, was taken in a debug build and overstated the cost by roughly eight to fifty times**; it is corrected rather than left, because a verification row that cites a measurement has to cite the right one. A second reading of the same code on the same day, by another session, independently measured 43 ms warm and 311 ms cold. It is one number on one machine and settles no threshold; it is the first figure [decision 0004](decisions/0004-preparation-is-real-generation-time.md)'s measure can rest on, since every timing before it was dominated by a tenth of a second of process startup.
